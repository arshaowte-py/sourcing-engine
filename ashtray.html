01 · The product & the call

What we're buying, and why it gets bought here

Specification to quote against

  • Shell: coated paper / cardboard cup, ~90 mm diameter, 3 cigarette rests, MAX fill line, glossy finish.
  • Fill: water-activated gel granules over an extinguishing-sand base — self-extinguishing, smokeless, odour-trapping.
  • Pack: the domestic listings ship in packs of 25; that's already the right case size for a café.
  • Branding: sleeve print — our logo, or the venue's for hotel white-label.
  • Confirm on every quote: gel set time, burn-through, odour hold after 3 hours, dry-granule shelf life, unit weight, case count, and whether gel ships loaded or user-activated.

The price picture, all in one place

  • ₹8/pc — Ahilya Enterprises, Badlapur: "Disposable Ashtray with Gel", cardboard + gel + paper, 90 mm. TrustSEAL, 8 yrs, 5★ from 102 ratings, 87% response.
  • ₹75/pc — Infinity Exim, Pune: same category, sold retail in packs of 25, 15-day delivery. A reseller price, not a factory one.
  • ₹42/pc — landed cost importing from China at the 48,000-piece tier.
  • $0.24–0.45/pc — what the West pays at retail in 25-packs, for reference on positioning.
  • The spread between ₹8 and ₹75 on the same product is the whole opportunity: nobody is running this as a branded offline supply business.
The call: procure domestically. At ₹8 landed-equivalent, selling to a restaurant at ₹18–25 gives 55–68% gross margin with zero customs exposure, zero forex, and reorders inside a week. Importing only becomes interesting past six-figure annual volumes, and even then the gain is small — it is a Year-2 question, kept on this page for reference and nothing more.

05 · Offline distribution

Who touches it, and what each hand takes

This is the part that decides whether the product works. A ₹40–50 consumable cannot carry a distributor, a retailer and an importer all at once — so we pick which layers to keep.

The margin ladder — 5-pack at ₹99 MRP

  • Consumer pays ₹99 for 5 → ₹19.80 a piece.
  • Retailer buys at ~₹72 (27% margin) → ₹14.40 a piece.
  • Distributor buys at ~₹65 (10%) → ₹13 a piece to us.
  • Against a delivered cost of ₹10.70 that's a thin 18% — workable but not exciting.
  • Made in-house at ₹5.50 it becomes 58%, and the retail pack turns into a real second channel. This is the clearest argument for Option B.

So the sequence is

  • Direct HORECA first. No distributor, no retailer — a case sold straight to a restaurant at ₹18–25 holds ~47% margin even on bought-in stock.
  • Prove weekly reorder across 15–20 venues. That number decides everything after it.
  • Then move to Option B if the volume supports a 10,000-piece production run — margin roughly doubles.
  • Then add the retail 5-pack for smoke shops and car accessory counters, which only pays properly once we're making it ourselves.
Honest caveat: the trade prices above are constructed from standard FMCG margin structures, not quotes from Indian distributors, and the ₹8 supply price is a listed slab, not a negotiated one. The first three HORECA conversations and one supplier call will replace both — those numbers, not this arithmetic, set the price list.
06 · The gate

One rule decides how we brand this

COTPA Section 5 — no tobacco branding, ours or anyone's

India's Cigarettes and Other Tobacco Products Act prohibits advertising of tobacco products directly or indirectly, and the rules explicitly treat using a tobacco brand name on other products as indirect advertising. Penalties run to imprisonment and fines, escalating on repeat offence.

What this means in practice. Selling an ashtray is legal and unrestricted — it is not a tobacco product. What we must never do is take a printing job that puts a cigarette or gutka brand on it, or market ours with imagery that promotes smoking. That kills one revenue line the Western promo trade relies on (tobacco-company giveaways) and leaves every other one open: cafés, bars, hotels, offices, events, car accessories, travel and general corporate gifting, all branded with their own logo or ours.

Action: a one-page brand-safety rule for the sales deck — we print client logos, never tobacco brands — plus a marketplace-policy check before listing (smoking accessories are permitted on Amazon.in, but listing rules should be read before the first upload). Not a legal opinion; worth 30 minutes with a lawyer before the B2B pitch goes out.

Why this works

  • It's a consumable. Every other product in this engine sells once; this reorders monthly from the same account.
  • Domestic supply means we can say yes to a 200-piece trial order — impossible on an import model.
  • The channel the boss named is exactly where direct selling beats the margin ladder.
  • Total spend to know whether this is a business: under ₹45K.

What could still kill it

  • The ₹8 might not hold. It's a listed slab price from one supplier; a real quote could come back at ₹15–20 and halve the margin.
  • No supplier lock-in. On Option A the same supplier can sell to anyone — which is exactly why Option B matters.
  • Reorder rate is unproven. A restaurant may use far fewer than 200 a month. Phase 1 exists to find out.
  • Quality on a job-work run. Gel set time and burn-through must be tested before any venue sees branded stock.
  • Collections. Selling to small venues on credit is how this kind of business quietly dies — cash or advance for the first three orders.
