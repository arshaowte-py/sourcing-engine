Teardown #3 · Copper import · India · Aug 2026

Copper, into India — at the worst possible moment to be a first-time buyer

India is the world's second-largest refined copper importer and brought in 1.2 million tonnes in FY2025. The trade is real, the demand is structural, and the margins are thin and technical. But copper is also sitting at record highs above $13,400 a tonne after the DRC concentrate export ban and a London warehouse squeeze — which means one 25-tonne container is roughly ₹3.2 crore of cargo. This page maps the forms, the countries, the duty, the compliance gates and the landed maths.

The market context nobody should skip

Copper is in a supply squeeze, not a normal market

As of 20 August 2026 copper trades around $6.48/lb (~$13,400–14,300/tonne), at or near all-time highs. Three things are driving it: the Democratic Republic of Congo banned concentrate exports, metal is being pulled toward the US ahead of possible tariffs on refined copper, and LME warehouse stocks have drained — at one point August futures traded $370 above September, the widest one-month spread since the 2021 squeeze. Chile, the largest producer, expects output to fall 2.6% this year.

What that means for us. Buying physical copper today means buying at the top of a violent market with a real chance of a sharp correction between the day we fix a price and the day the container lands. Copper trades are priced off LME and settle weeks later — the price risk between contract and delivery is the single largest number on this page, and it dwarfs duty, freight and margin combined. Nobody should enter this trade without deciding first how the LME exposure gets hedged or passed through to the buyer.

Cathode is a cleaner product but the BIS gate makes supplier selection brutally narrow.

Where I'd actually start

  • UAE for the first container. A week's transit means a mistake costs days, not months, and CEPA plus proximity keeps the total cost of learning low.
  • Japan for cathode, if cathode is the plan. CEPA tariff relief plus documentation quality offsets the premium — but only if the supplier already holds BIS.
  • USA/Germany for graded scrap once we can absorb a 30–40 day cycle and want consistent ISRI grades.
  • Skip DRC entirely for now regardless of price. Export policy is live and moving against buyers.

How copper is actually priced

  • Everything indexes to LME cash. Nobody quotes a flat price — they quote LME plus or minus a differential.
  • Cathode = LME + physical premium (origin, brand and delivery point set the premium).
  • Scrap = a percentage of LME by ISRI grade: Berry/Millberry bare bright wire at the top, Birch/Cliff and Candy below it, insulated grades lower again.
  • So the negotiation is about the differential and the pricing window, not the headline price. Which QP (quotation period) applies is the most commercially important clause in the contract.

The number that actually matters: because IGST comes back as input credit, the true irrecoverable duty burden is roughly 5.5% on cathode and 0–2.75% on scrap. IGST still has to be funded at clearance though — on a ₹3.2 crore container that's about ₹60 lakh of working capital tied up until the credit is claimed. Cash flow, not tax, is the constraint.

FTA preference can take BCD toward zero on qualifying origins (Japan CEPA, UAE CEPA, Chile PTA) — but only with a valid Certificate of Origin filed at clearance, and rules of origin must genuinely be met. Verify the specific 8-digit line before pricing a deal on a preferential rate.

04 · Compliance

Four gates — and one of them stops most people

NFMIMS
Non-Ferrous Metals Import Monitoring System. Mandatory registration for all Chapter 74 copper imports under DGFT Notification 61/2015-20.
Applies to: every form of copper. Register no earlier than 60 days and no later than 5 days before the consignment arrives; you receive a URN starting "NFS". Missing it invites action under the FTDR Act — this is a hard timing rule, not paperwork to catch up on later.
BIS / QCO
Ministry of Mines Quality Control Order: imported copper cathodes must conform to IS 191:2007 and carry the ISI mark.
Applies to: cathode, and notified finished products. This is the gate that kills most cathode plans — the foreign producer's plant must hold a BIS licence, and industry has publicly called the norms restrictive on supply. Confirm the exact mill is BIS-listed before negotiating anything.
PSIC
Pre-Shipment Inspection Certificate from a DGFT-recognised agency, generated online through the DGFT portal (mandatory online since 1 July 2022).
Applies to: metal scrap. Certificates not generated through the DGFT system may be rejected at customs. Scrap from designated "safe" countries can be exempt if imported through specified ports — check both lists before booking.
PCB & radiation
Pollution Control Board clearance for waste and scrap, plus the radioactive-contamination checking SOP under DGFT Public Notice 37/2015-20.
Applies to: scrap. Scrap clearance is permitted only at specified ports, and consignments are subject to random radiation screening. Port choice is a compliance decision, not a freight one.

Base registrations

  • IEC from DGFT for the importing entity.
  • GST registration — without it the 18% IGST becomes a dead cost instead of a credit.
  • AD Code registered with our bank at each port we clear through.
  • Customs broker (CHA) experienced specifically in Chapter 74 — classification here has real money attached.
  • Most copper moves on LC terms; the bank line has to exist before a supplier will engage seriously.

Ports that matter

  • JNPT / Nhava Sheva and Mundra handle the highest metal volumes.
  • Chennai is the natural east-coast option for a Bangalore base — roughly 350 km by road.
  • Kandla and Visakhapatnam also active for copper.
  • Free storage at major ports runs about 3–7 days for FCL. After that demurrage accrues daily — on a multi-container copper shipment, a documentation delay can erase the entire margin inside two weeks.

This is a working-capital and risk-management business, not a sourcing-and-markup business like everything else in this engine.
06 · Honest assessment

What makes this hard

Real risks, in order of severity

  • Price risk dominates everything. Copper is at record highs in a documented squeeze. A 5% correction between contract and delivery is ₹17 lakh on one container — larger than any realistic trading margin.
  • Working capital. ₹3.4 crore per container, plus ₹60 lakh of IGST funded until credit flows. Needs an LC line, not a bank balance.
  • Counterparty fraud. Metal trading attracts documentary fraud — false bills of lading, cargo that doesn't exist. Non-negotiable: LC terms, an independent inspection agency, and a supplier with verifiable trade history.
  • Quality disputes. Scrap is graded subjectively; a load downgraded on arrival can turn a profit into a loss with no recourse if the contract is loose.
  • Regulatory rejection. BIS on cathode, PSIC on scrap. A container that can't clear is a container accruing demurrage.

What would make it work

  • A confirmed buyer before a purchase order — back-to-back, so we never hold unsold metal. This is how most small metal traders survive.
  • Start with scrap, not cathode — lower duty, no BIS gate, smaller lots.
  • Start with UAE — a week's transit means the feedback loop on our first mistake is short.
  • Price back-to-back on the same LME window so the differential is the margin and the flat price is passed through.
  • One container as tuition. Treat the first shipment as a paid education in documentation, clearance and grading — priced accordingly.
Where this sits against the rest of the engine: the ashtray needs ₹45,000 to find out whether it's a business. Copper needs ₹3.4 crore, an LC line and an LME view before the first container moves. Both can be good decisions — they are not the same kind of decision, and shouldn't be evaluated with the same appetite.

What I need to take this further

  1. 01Which form? Scrap, cathode or wire rod — everything above branches from this and I've assumed scrap is the realistic entry.
  2. 02Trading or consuming? Are we importing to resell, or feeding a plant with a known monthly requirement? Back-to-back trading and inventory buying are different businesses.
  3. 03What volume and what capital? One container is ₹3.4 crore of cathode or roughly ₹2.5–3 crore of scrap. Is there an LC line, and for how much?
  4. 04Is there a buyer already? If a named Indian smelter or cable maker will commit to offtake, this becomes a far better proposition and the country shortlist narrows immediately.
